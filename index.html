How I'd position Osmos as the Retail Media Operating System for omnichannel growth.

MY THESIS

Retailers don't have a demand problem. They have an infrastructure problem. Legacy ad stacks make it difficult to scale media across onsite, offsite, and in-store channels while maintaining the commerce context that makes retail media valuable. As a result, many retail media networks struggle to scale beyond ~1% of GMV.

Competitive Strategy

Winning on composability, sub-20ms latency, and data sovereignty. Conceding legacy agency brokerages.

Why enterprise retailers choose sovereign first-party infrastructure over third-party ad networks.

Differentiating from Criteo Retail Media Data Sovereignty & Direct Brand Trust
Concede: Criteo dominates legacy demand pools and extensive global DSP networks.
Reframe: Retailers need private-label infrastructure to retain 100% first-party shopper data sovereignty.
Proof: Osmos delivers white-labeled direct billing and self-serve consoles without third-party data leakage.
Differentiating from Kevel Turnkey Operating System vs. Raw APIs
Concede: Kevel offers flexible API primitives for custom ad-server engineering.
Reframe: Raw APIs require 12+ months dev time to build brand UI, reporting, and billing.
Proof: Osmos deploys turnkey self-serve portals, billing, and Sofie AI yield automation in 4 weeks.
Differentiating from CitrusAd (Publicis) Platform Neutrality & Engineering Independence
Concede: CitrusAd brings captive agency spend via Publicis Groupe holding ties.
Reframe: Retailers avoid agency-biased black boxes that favor specific holding groups.
Proof: Osmos provides neutral adtech infrastructure with sub-20ms SLAs across all agency partners.
